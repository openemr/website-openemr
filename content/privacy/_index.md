The OpenEMR Foundation, Inc. is a U.S. 501(c)(3) nonprofit that supports the OpenEMR open-source project. This policy explains, in plain language, what information we and the services we rely on collect when you use our website and community forum, why, and what choices you have.

The short version: we don't sell your information, we don't run advertising, and the only optional tracking is Google Analytics, which stays off unless you click **Accept**.

## What this policy covers

This policy covers:

- **www.open-emr.org**, including the project wiki at www.open-emr.org/wiki/
- **The community forum** at community.open-emr.org

It does **not** cover:

- **The online demos** at demo.openemr.io. These are public test systems that reset every day. Never enter real patient information into a demo.
- **Sites we link to**, including donation platforms, GitHub, OpenCoreEMR and other partners. Once you leave our sites, that site's own privacy policy applies.

OpenEMR is software that clinics install and run themselves. The Foundation does not host anyone's medical records and never receives patient information through our sites. Please don't post health information about yourself or anyone else on the wiki or the forum.

## Information collected when you visit

### Server and network logs

Like nearly every website, ours are delivered through several infrastructure services. Each one automatically receives the technical details your browser sends with every request: your IP address, the page you asked for, the date and time, the referring page, and your browser and device type.

- **Cloudflare** sits in front of www.open-emr.org to speed it up and block attacks.
- **GitHub Pages** hosts most of the website's pages.
- **DigitalOcean** hosts the server that runs the wiki and routes traffic to GitHub Pages. That server keeps standard web access logs.
- **Amazon Web Services (AWS)** hosts the forum.

We use these logs only to keep our sites running, secure and free of abuse. We keep our own server logs only as long as needed for those purposes, and we don't use them to profile individual visitors.

### Analytics (only if you accept)

If you click **Accept** on the cookie banner, we use **Google Analytics 4** to understand which pages are useful and how people find us. Google Analytics then sets cookies and collects information such as the pages you view, how long you stay, your approximate location (city or country level), your browser and device type, and the site that referred you.

We've configured it to collect as little as practical:

- Google signals and ad personalization are turned off, so this data isn't used for advertising.
- Data is kept for 2 months and then deleted.
- According to Google, Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses.

If you click **Decline**, or never click either button, Google Analytics is not loaded at all. We also never run it on wiki login and account pages.

The website and the wiki share one banner, so one choice covers both. The forum has its own banner, and your choice there is saved separately. You can change your mind at any time: on the website or wiki, use the **Cookie settings** link at the bottom of the page; on the forum, use the forum's cookie settings.

### Content loaded from other services

To display the website, your browser fetches some files directly from other providers. That means those providers receive your IP address and browser details:

- **Fonts** from Google Fonts and Adobe Fonts
- **Code libraries** from jsDelivr, cdnjs (Cloudflare), the Bootstrap CDN and the jQuery CDN
- **Videos** embedded from YouTube on the homepage, the lectures pages and a few blog posts. We use YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode, so YouTube doesn't set cookies unless you play a video.
- **Videos on some wiki pages**, embedded from YouTube and similar services. The video service receives your details when the video loads.

### Search engine data

We use Google Search Console to see how the website appears in Google search results. It reports totals from Google's side, like search terms and click counts, and doesn't collect anything from you on our sites.

## Information you choose to give us

### Wiki accounts

Anyone can read the wiki. To edit, you need an account, which wiki administrators create on request; anonymous editing is turned off. For each account we store your username, your password (in a scrambled, hashed form) and your email address if you provide one. When you're logged in, the wiki uses cookies to keep you signed in.

- **Edits and uploads are public.** The page history shows your username next to each change, and uploaded files can be seen by anyone, including any details embedded in the file (such as where and when a photo was taken).
- **The wiki software records the network address of each edit** for about 90 days to help fight spam and abuse. It isn't shown publicly.
- **Email:** the wiki sends account emails (such as password resets) and talk-page notifications from hello@open-emr.org. If you use the wiki's "Email this user" feature, the person you write to will see your email address.

### Forum accounts and posts

The forum runs on Discourse. If you create a forum account, we store:

- Your **username, email address and password** (in a scrambled, hashed form). If you sign up with an outside account such as GitHub or Facebook, we receive basic profile details from that service, such as your name, email address and profile picture, and we never see your password for that service.
- **Anything you add to your profile**, such as a name, picture, bio or location.
- **Your posts and messages.** Posts, your username and your public profile can be seen by anyone on the internet. Private messages can be seen by the people you send them to, and forum administrators can access them when needed to deal with abuse or spam.
- **The IP addresses** you sign up and log in from. Forum moderators and administrators can see these to fight spam and abuse.

The forum sends emails, such as account confirmations, notifications and summaries, using **Amazon Simple Email Service (SES)**. You can choose which emails you get in your forum preferences.

### Donations

Donations are handled by **Zeffy**, **GitHub Sponsors** and **Open Collective**. You give your payment details directly to those platforms, and the Foundation never sees or stores card numbers. The platform shares with us what's needed to thank you, issue receipts and keep nonprofit records, such as your name, email address, the amount and date, and any message you include. Each platform's own privacy policy also applies.

## Cookies

| Cookie | Set by | Purpose | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cf_clearance` | Cloudflare | Security: remembers that your browser passed a Cloudflare security check, so you aren't checked again | Only after you're shown a security check; lasts up to 1 year |
| Wiki session, user ID and username cookies | Our wiki (MediaWiki) | Keeps you logged in | Only if you log in to the wiki; up to 30 days, or up to 180 days if you choose "Keep me logged in" |
| `UseDC` | Our wiki (MediaWiki) | Technical: makes sure you see your own edits right away | Briefly, after you save a wiki edit |
| `_t`, `_forum_session` | Our forum (Discourse) | Keeps you logged in and remembers your session | When you use the forum; the login cookie lasts until you log out or it expires |
| `_ga`, `_ga_*` | Google Analytics | Analytics | Only after you click **Accept**; up to 13 months |

Your analytics choice is remembered by your browser so the banner doesn't reappear on every page. Our sites don't use advertising or cross-site tracking cookies.

## How we use and share information

We use information only to run, secure and improve our sites and the OpenEMR project, to send forum emails you've chosen to receive, and to process and acknowledge donations.

**We do not sell or rent personal information, and we don't share it for advertising.** We share it only with the service providers named in this policy, so they can do their jobs for us, or when the law requires it.

## Where information is processed

The Foundation and most of our service providers are based in the United States. If you visit from elsewhere, including the EU, UK, India or Nigeria, your information will be processed in the United States and in other countries where those providers operate.

## Your choices and rights

- **Analytics:** decline on the banner, or change your choice anytime on each site.
- **Forum:** you can edit your profile and email settings, and download a copy of your posts and account data, from your forum preferences.
- **Wiki:** you can update your account settings. For other requests, contact us.
- **Access, correction and deletion:** you can ask what personal information we hold about you, or ask us to correct or delete it. If you ask us to delete your forum account, we may keep your posts with your name removed so that discussions still make sense. Public wiki history and records we're legally required to keep, such as donation records, may also be exceptions.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights under laws such as the EU and UK GDPR, U.S. state privacy laws, India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, or the Nigeria Data Protection Act. These can include the right to object to processing, to withdraw consent, or to complain to your local data protection authority. Contact us and we'll respond within the time the law requires.

## Children

Our sites are meant for people who work with or develop OpenEMR. They aren't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children.

## Security

We use HTTPS across our sites and rely on reputable infrastructure providers. No website can guarantee perfect security, but we limit what we collect so there's less to protect.
